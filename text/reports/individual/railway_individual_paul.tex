\section{Contributions}
\subsection{Ideation}
I had the idea of encoding energy costs, which we agreed to be our topic. As these costs depend a lot on the speed of a train, I introduced the idea of encoding variable speed. I also had the idea of using heuristics instead of optimization in the acceleration encoding to reduce solving time.

\subsection{Programming}
We agreed to use my encoding for reaching a goal as a baseline encoding for our team. I also coded all of the acceleration and energy cost logic, including heuristics in the acceleration encoding.

\subsection{Debugging}
As I did the programming, I was mainly responsible for the debugging too.
\subsection{Presentation}

I presented the progress of our encodings in the weekly meetings. In the final presentation I talked about our example environment results and conclusions.

\section{Challenges}
As it is hard to foresee the impact of encoding or environment changes on the solving time, I had problems to find an environment for different encodings, which kept solving time reasonable while still being complex enough to show different behaviors. Also the maxtime has a huge impact on solving time and the environments we could generate were not applicable to temporary speeds in that regard (as their maxtimes were too low). I had to run a lot of tests to determine the lowest maxtime that is still high enough to keep the environment satisfiable.

\section{Recommendations}
As raw outputs of clingo runs are hard to read, I would recommend to use some sort of visualization to support that. Also, future students should definitely look into heuristics if they have an encoding without optimization. Even a simple heuristic using basic domain knowledge can reduce the time to find the first satisfying model by more than 50 percent (just make sure to use the {-}{-}heuristic=domain flag).
