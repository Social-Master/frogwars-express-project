\section{Conclusion}
In this project, we extended the railway scheduling problem within the Flatland framework by modeling energy consumption using Answer Set Programming (ASP). Our results demonstrate that the ASP encoding accurately captures physical realities at the local level: energy costs dynamically scale based on vehicle mass, power type, and specific kinematic states.

However, our current ASP encoding exhibits limitations regarding computational scalability. The introduction of dynamic speed choices expands the search space exponentially, because the solver must branch through many kinematic combinations across extended time horizons to find a solution satisfying the arrival times.

We consider several extensions and modifications for future work based on our findings:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Algorithmic Improvements:} To manage the exponential growth of the search space on larger grids, future research could employ different solving techniques, such as Incremental Solving. Osswald and Leiber (2026) successfully employed this strategy to minimize the sum of arrival times of all their agents for multiple environments, which was successful for a majority of their testing environments. However, as they mention, this method "only reduces the time horizon and, therefore, may not be suitable for all optimization metrics." (\cite{osswald_railway_2026}).
    \item \textbf{Environmental Physics:} With dynamic acceleration and momentum mechanics now established, future iterations could take track elevation and gradients/slopes into account, adding another layer of realism to how trains consume power on inclines or recover energy on declines.
    \item \textbf{Alternative Optimization Strategies:} Creating and combining minimize directives and/or the heuristics, or adjusting the existing ones, could allow the solver to actively evaluate and choose longer, more energy efficient detours rather than strictly defaulting to the fastest geometric route.
\end{itemize}

Overall, we provide a robust proof of concept that ASP can effectively model physical constraints in railway logistics, laying the groundwork for future research into multi-objective routing.
