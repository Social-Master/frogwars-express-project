\subsection{Results}
For all trial runs a single environment was used. It consists of a 25 $\times$ 25 network with two stations and four agents. Three of the agents move at the speed of 1, the remaining one at speed 4.
\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{./images/environment.png}
  \caption{Flatland environment used for all experiments}
  \label{fig:environment_image}
\end{figure}

The baseline encoding was only used to establish rules for bringing trains from a starting point to an end point with a proper behavior for a given environment. That goal was reached successfully: The baseline encoding successfully generated paths for all four agents from their starting point to their end points (see tables \ref{tab:baseline0}, \ref{tab:baseline1}, \ref{tab:baseline2} and \ref{tab:baseline3}).

Extending the baseline encoding with the rules and heuristics to model energy consumption and efficiency resulted in the encodings no longer working properly: None of the trains reached their end points, and trains 1 and 2 even ended up on the same track traveling in opposite directions, such that they were not able to continue on (see tables \ref{tab:energy0}, \ref{tab:energy1}, \ref{tab:energy2} and \ref{tab:energy3}).
